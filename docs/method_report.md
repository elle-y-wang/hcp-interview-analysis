# Method report — multimodal analysis of HCP interviews

Covers framework §6.3 ("items the methods section must report"). Written to be
lifted into a manuscript with light editing. Numbers marked `[auto]` are
regenerated by the scripts and should be re-read from the output tables after any
re-run.

**Study**: Emergency Department Peer Support Worker Visibility Study. 30
recorded interviews with healthcare providers about a peer support worker (PSW)
program introduced into a hospital emergency department.

Site and ethics-approval details are deliberately omitted from this public
repository.

---

## 0. Three deviations from the original framework

The framework was written against a set of assumptions that did not all hold for
this dataset. Three departures follow, each deliberate and described where it
occurs.

| # | Framework assumed | Actually true | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Focus groups, one unit per session | Filenames say `HCPFocusGroup` and interviewers say "focus group", but sessions diarize to **2 speakers** with a single consenting participant — these are one-on-one interviews, **except four sessions** carrying two consenting participants each | Framework's L1/L2/L3 structure applies, with participant IDs assigned per speaker so paired sessions stay distinct |
| 2 | `participants.csv` and `interview_guide.md` supplied | Neither was in the folder initially | Guide reconstructed from spoken section cues (`docs/interview_guide.md`); participant IDs recovered from spoken consent. Demographics were supplied later (34 rows) and RQ3 is in scope |
| 3 | WhisperX + pyannote for diarization | pyannote checkpoints are gated and no HF token is available | Diarization rebuilt from ungated cam++ embeddings + spectral clustering |

All audio is English (30/30 sessions, language-ID p ≥ 0.92). Where the framework
named a model without specifying a language, the English-trained equivalent is
used; each substitution is noted at the step where it occurs.

---

## 1. Audio preparation and quality

All recordings converted to 16 kHz mono 16-bit WAV (`scripts/01_prepare_audio.py`).

- **Corpus**: 30 sessions, 12.75 h total, 12.5–53.2 min each (mean 25.5 min).
- **Two recording conditions**, which is a confound the framework explicitly
  warns about (§1.1, §8):

  | condition | n | codec / rate / channels |
  |---|---|---|
  | A | 13 | AAC 32 kHz mono, ~127 kbps |
  | B | 17 | MP3 44.1 kHz stereo, ~192 kbps |

  `recording_device` is carried through to the mixed model as a covariate.
- **Clipping**: none detected (clip ratio 0.000 in all 30).
- **Estimated SNR is not comparable across conditions.** Condition A returns
  60–102 dB and condition B 20–34 dB. This does *not* mean A is cleaner: the
  A recordings contain digitally silent passages (noise gating in the capture
  app), which makes the noise-floor percentile collapse. It is a property of the
  device, not of the room.
- **No denoising was applied.** Framework §8 warns that denoising a subset
  introduces systematic bias; since the quality difference tracks the device
  rather than a few bad sessions, and ASR quality was acceptable throughout,
  uniform no-denoising was preferred.
- Stereo sources were downmixed to mono. L/R correlation is 0.82–0.97
  (mean 0.90), i.e. genuinely two-channel but highly redundant; nothing
  speaker-separable is lost by the downmix.

Outputs: `data/sessions.csv`, `data/audio_qc.csv`.

## 2. Transcription

- **Model**: faster-whisper `large-v3`, `int8_float16`, CUDA (RTX 2060, 6 GB).
- **Settings**: `language="en"`, `beam_size=5`, word timestamps on, VAD filter on
  (`min_silence_duration_ms=500`), `condition_on_previous_text=False` (prevents
  runaway repetition on long recordings).
- **Throughput**: real-time factor ≈ 0.07–0.11.
- **Corpus**: 112,970 words transcribed, of which 79,133 are participant speech.

**Transcription accuracy has NOT been spot-checked.** Framework §1.2 requires
5 minutes per session to be manually verified and an error rate reported. This
requires a human listener and is outstanding. Known systematic error: "ED" is
frequently rendered "EU", "AD" or "EV"; this is normalised before topic-cue
matching but remains in the stored transcript text.

## 3. Speaker diarization

pyannote was unavailable (gated), so diarization is:

1. speech regions from Whisper word timestamps;
2. 1.5 s windows, 0.75 s hop, one 192-d **cam++** speaker embedding each
   (`iic/speech_campplus_sv_zh-cn_16k-common`; speaker embeddings are
   language-independent);
3. cosine affinity → row-wise pruning → normalised Laplacian → **eigengap**
   chooses speaker count → k-means on leading eigenvectors;
4. temporal median smoothing; each word takes the nearest window's label.

**Speaker count was estimated, never assumed** — this is what established that
the recordings are two-person, and it is also what caught the exception: **four
sessions have three speakers and two consenting participants each**. One further
session yields a spurious ~0.5 s third cluster. Per-session counts are written to
`data/role_assignment_check.csv`, which stays out of this repository.

Two consequences follow, both handled in `scripts/04_build_utterances.py`:
- **Study IDs are assigned per speaker, not per session.** Assigning one ID per
  recording would have silently merged each paired session's two participants
  into a single L3 unit and corrupted both the participant-level table and the
  random-intercept grouping.
- Speakers holding <2% of the non-interviewer speech with no study ID are
  labelled `unassigned` and excluded, so clustering artefacts do not become
  phantom participants.

**Study IDs are spoken, so they inherit the ASR's errors.** Five distinct
mis-renderings appear across the 30 recordings, each of which silently dropped or
corrupted a participant before being handled:

| spoken form as transcribed | recovered as | cause |
|---|---|---|
| `PN-0nn`, `PN0nn`, `pn0nn` | as written | — |
| `P p.m. 0nn` | `PN0nn` | "PN" heard as "p.m." |
| `P-N-O-nn`, `PNOnn` | `PN0nn` | spoken zero written as letter **O** |
| `PIN 0nn` | `PN0nn` | "PN" heard as the word "pin" |
| `PN00nn8` | `PN0nn` | spurious trailing digits |
| `study ID is PN` … `study id 0nn` | `PN0nn` | number lands in a later turn |
| `my study iv is pn-0nn` | `PN0nn` | "study ID" heard as "study iv" |

*(Digits masked as `nn`. The session-to-ID mapping is deliberately not recorded
in this repository — see §5.)*

Handling: the prefix is matched loosely, `O`/`o` is folded to zero, only the
first three digits are trusted, and a second-tier pattern catches IDs separated
from their prefix. Any ID recovered through a fallback is flagged
`id_needs_review` in `data/role_assignment_check.csv`.

**This matters more than it looks.** The zero-as-O error alone hid a second
participant in one session; left unhandled it would have merged two people into
one and attached one person's demographics to the other's transcript, which no
downstream check would catch.

**The recovery has since been verified against the demographics export**: 34 IDs
from audio, 34 rows in the spreadsheet, no unmatched record on either side
(§9b).

**Role assignment** used two independent signals, not a default:
- the speaker who reads the recording slate ("for the audio recording today is…")
  is the interviewer;
- the speaker who reads the verbal consent ("my name is …, my study ID is PN0xx")
  is the participant.

Sessions where these disagree are flagged in `data/role_assignment_check.csv`.
**Diarization error rate has not been measured against a human reference** —
outstanding, same category as transcription spot-checking.

## 4. Analysis units

| level | unit | definition |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | utterance | a speaker-homogeneous, sentence-like span (a Whisper segment, split further wherever the diarizer changes speaker) |
| L2 | topic segment | one of 6 guide sections |
| L3 | participant | 34 people across 30 recordings (4 sessions are paired interviews) |

The conversational turn was rejected as the L1 unit: merging all consecutive
same-speaker speech yields only ~2.7 k units for 12.75 h, far too coarse for the
5–7 unit sliding window in framework §5.2. The sentence-like unit yields
**8,398 units** (5,378 of them participant speech, 5,082 after backchannels are
excluded, mean 5.6 s), inside the framework's own expected 5,000–10,000 range.
`turn_index` is retained so turn structure is still available.

**Participants: 34, not 30.** Twenty-four sessions diarize to two speakers and
six to three; of the latter, four are genuine paired interviews and two are
clustering artefacts. Study IDs form a contiguous run of 34 with no gaps,
which is a useful internal check: 30 sessions + 4 second participants = 34, and a
contiguous sequence means no ID was mis-parsed into a wrong slot.

Utterances shorter than 1 s or under 3 words are flagged `is_backchannel=1`,
kept on the timeline and excluded from all emotion analysis (framework §1.2).

## 5. De-identification

Participants speak their real names during verbal consent. Policy applied:

- `participant_id` is the spoken **study ID** (`PN###`), recovered by regex from
  the consent statement — so the analysis key comes from the audio itself.
- All PERSON spans are replaced with `[NAME]` in every stored transcript, using
  the consent-formula regex plus `dslim/bert-base-NER` over every utterance.
  This also catches third parties named in the interviews (colleagues, the PSWs
  themselves, the PI).
- The name↔ID mapping is written only to `data/private/name_map.csv`.
  **Exclude `data/private/` from any sharing, repository, or supplement.**

## 6. Acoustic features (L1)

- **Interpretable set**: openSMILE **eGeMAPSv02** functionals. Reported subset:
  F0 mean / p20 / p80 / coefficient of variation, loudness mean and p80, jitter,
  shimmer, HNR, voiced segments per second, mean voiced and unvoiced segment
  length. Speech rate is `words_per_s` from word timestamps.
- **Deep model**: `audeering/wav2vec2-large-robust-12-ft-emotion-msp-dim`,
  giving continuous **arousal / dominance / valence** on 0–1. Continuous
  dimensions are used rather than discrete emotion labels because framework §8
  warns those are uninformative in low-arousal interview speech. emotion2vec is
  not used here.
- **Within-speaker z-scoring (framework §2.1, mandatory)**: every feature is
  z-scored using that speaker's own mean and SD. The grouping key is
  **(session, speaker)**, not participant — each recording contains an
  interviewer as well, and pooling them would fold the interviewer's baseline
  pitch and level into the participant's. This normalisation simultaneously
  removes speaker baseline, gender-linked F0, and the two recording conditions.

**Sanity check (framework §2.3)**: arousal must correlate positively with F0 and
loudness. An earlier version grouped the z-scores by session and produced
`corr(arousal, F0) = −0.26`; switching to per-speaker grouping fixed it. Current
values are reported by the script and are positive.

## 6b. Topic segmentation (L2)

The framework offers manual marking or an LLM pass. A third route was available
and is more auditable: interviewers announce each transition out loud. Cues are
matched against interviewer speech only, forced into guide order.

Two failure modes had to be fixed, and the second was severe:

- **Wording varies far more than expected.** "the next section is…", "these next
  few questions will be related to…", "the next set of questions will be
  regarding…", "our next section here is…". A narrow cue list found boundaries in
  only 10 of 30 sessions.
- **Cues straddle utterance boundaries.** L1 units are sentence-sized, so a
  transition routinely splits in half ("…the next set of questions will be
  regarding challenges and a" + "reas for improvement"). Matching per utterance
  cannot see these at all. Interviewer speech is therefore concatenated into one
  stream with a char-offset→timestamp index, and cues are matched against the
  stream, then mapped back to a timestamp.

After both fixes: **22 of 30 sessions segment completely (5/5 boundaries)**, all
30 have `experiences`, `challenges` and `sustainability`, 28 have `services`, and
18 have an explicit `closing`. Sessions missing `closing` simply end without the
scripted wrap-up.

Ordered matching also guards a real false-positive risk: before the first
question the interviewer reads a scripted description of the PSW role that
contains both "services" and "challenges". Because each section is only searched
for *after* the previous boundary, the boilerplate cannot trigger one.

Coverage per session is in `data/topic_boundaries.csv` together with the exact
sentence that triggered each boundary — the worksheet for the 20% spot-check.

## 7. Text analysis (L1)

- **Sentiment** (§3.2): `cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest`,
  3-class, ungated. `tx_valence = p(pos) − p(neg)`,
  plus label and a stance field. z-scored within speaker.
- **Thematic coding** (§3.1): codebook in `docs/codebook.md` — **v2, 35 codes in
  five domains**. v1 was built deductively from the guide plus inductive reading
  of two transcripts. v2 was rewritten against the team's own preliminary analysis
  (*HCP FG Preliminary Analysis, 11 Sept 2025*, Table 4) so that:
  the terminology matches the team's ("peer", "peer programme", not "PSW");
  the domain structure follows their Table 4; and code wording is taken verbatim
  from Table 4 wherever a code exists there. The point is that this analysis and
  the manual one share a vocabulary instead of running as two parallel schemes.
  Table 4's ~100 codes include a long tail appearing once each, which is too
  sparse for reliable automated coding, so codes at ≥10% prevalence are kept
  close to verbatim and rarer ones fold into their parent.
  Applied by an LLM (`claude` CLI, headless) over consecutive utterances with
  interviewer turns retained as context.

  **Scope differs from the team's preliminary analysis and the percentages will
  not match.** Table 4 covers 20 of 34 participants (17 transcripts, limited by
  time). This pipeline codes all 34 participants across all 30 recordings.
  `direct_peer_contact` in `data/participants.csv` reproduces a comparable
  subset if a like-for-like check is wanted.
  **Demographics were deliberately withheld from the coding prompt**, departing
  from the framework's template: its own §8 lists feeding demographics to the
  coder as the trap that makes later group comparison circular, and RQ3 is
  exactly such a comparison.

**Coding reliability has NOT been established.** Framework §3.1 step C requires
300–500 utterances double-coded by two researchers and a Krippendorff's α
(MASI distance for multi-label). This needs humans. `data/utterances_coded.csv`
carries a per-utterance `code_confidence` in the meantime, which is not a
substitute.

## 8. Fusion (L1)

Scheme A (framework §4.1), since the sample cannot support a trained fusion model:

```
fused_valence = w_a · ac_valence_z + w_t · tx_valence_z     (w_a = w_t = 0.5)
fused_arousal = ac_arousal_z                                 (no text channel)
```

**Sensitivity analysis** over w_a ∈ [0, 1] is in
`outputs/tables/fusion_sensitivity.csv`; the topic ranking is reported there and
should be quoted in the manuscript.

**Discordant utterances (§4.3)**: flagged when text and acoustic valence have
opposite signs and both |z| > 0.5, sub-typed into
`positive_words_flat_voice` and `negative_words_bright_voice`. These are the
highest-value utterances for qualitative reading — framework §8 warns that
discarding them as noise is a classic error.

## 9. Validation status (framework §4.4) — OUTSTANDING

`data/annotation_sample.csv` is generated: a stratified sample across
participants, topic segments and valence terciles, with all discordant
utterances force-included, and blank columns for two raters.

Until two humans complete it while listening to the audio, **none** of the
following can be reported: inter-rater agreement; model-vs-human agreement;
whether fusion beats either single modality. Any claim that the emotion measures
are validated on this dataset would be unsupported.

## 9b. Demographics (RQ3)

`scripts/00_prepare_demographics.py` builds `data/participants.csv` from
*Phase II HCP Demographic Data.xlsx*.

**The file reconciles exactly with the audio: 34 rows, 34 participants recovered
from spoken consent, zero IDs on either side without a match.** Since the IDs
were recovered by transcribing people reading them aloud — through several ASR
mangles (§5) — this is a meaningful independent check that the recovery is
correct. Derived statistics also reproduce the team's Table 1 exactly: median age
33.5 (Q1–Q3 28–40, range 21–66), 24 female / 10 male.

Professions are Nurse 12, Security 6, Social Worker 5, Physician 5, EMED RA 4,
Pharmacist 2. Framework §5.3 requires ≥5 per cell for a group test, so
`occupation_category` pools the two smallest into "Pharmacy & research" (n=6),
giving five groups of 5–12. The ungrouped `occupation` column is kept for
descriptive reporting.

Free-text demographic answers are written to
`data/private/participants_freetext.csv` rather than the analysis table: in a
sample this small, a free-text answer about someone's role can re-identify them.

## 10. Statistical models (§5.3)

Mixed effects with a participant random intercept, random slopes omitted
(34 participants). ICC reported.

**Two models are fitted, not one.** The framework asks for topic, gender,
profession and recording device in a single model on the fused score. That
cannot work here, and the reason is worth stating plainly: §2.1's within-speaker
z-scoring sets every participant's own mean to zero, so a predictor that is
constant within a person has almost nothing left to explain. Between-person SD
of the person means is 0.07 for valence and 0.13 for arousal, against a
within-person SD of 0.87; ICC is 0.000. Fitting profession and device on the
fused score anyway produced coefficients of exactly 0.0 with 95% CIs of ±1e7 —
a degenerate fit that the first version of this pipeline wrote into the
coefficient table and plotted as though it were a result.

So the question is split to match where the variance actually is:

```
within   fused_valence  ~ C(topic_segment) * C(gender)                       # ICC 0.000
between  ac_valence     ~ C(gender) + C(occupation_category) + C(recording_device)   # ICC 0.157
within   fused_arousal  ~ C(topic_segment) * C(gender)                       # ICC 0.002
between  ac_arousal     ~ C(gender) + C(occupation_category) + C(recording_device)   # ICC 0.458
```

The between-person models use the **raw, uncentred** acoustic scores, which is
the only place person-level differences survive. Every row of
`outputs/tables/mixed_model_coefficients.csv` carries a `scope` column saying
which model it came from; the two are on different scales and must not be read
against each other. Any term whose CI is non-finite or wider than 10 units is
dropped rather than reported.

The topic × gender interaction is identified in the within model (it asks
whether men and women *shift* by different amounts, not whether they differ in
level) but remains **exploratory**: 34 participants detects only
medium-to-large effects.

Two cautions on the between-person results. `recording_device` is itself
significant for raw arousal (−0.089, p=0.003), so it is a measurement
confound, not a finding — and any profession that was recorded predominantly on
one setup is partly confounded with it, even though device is adjusted for.
Second, raw acoustic scores carry voice and speaking style, not only affect, so
"Security staff scored lower on raw arousal" is a statement about the
measurement as much as about how they felt. Both are flagged on Figure 8.

Framework §8's warning is honoured — utterance-level results are never treated as
independent observations; all group comparisons run through the mixed model or
through participant-level aggregates.

## 11. Positioning of the quantitative results

Per the analysis brief, this study is **qualitative-primary**. Themes, quotations
and case readings are the findings. The emotion measures serve to locate
passages worth close reading — especially discordant utterances — and to
describe topic-level patterns. They are not offered as validated affect
measurement, and §9 above is why.
